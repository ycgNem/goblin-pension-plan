# Goblin Pension Plan — Art Overhaul Plan

> The game plays well and reads clearly. It looks like a prototype. This plan replaces the
> code-drawn art with generated pixel art, map by map, without changing how anything plays.

**Budget:** at most **350 PixelLab generations** for the whole overhaul, tracked in the [ledger](#ledger) at the bottom.
**Rule:** every phase ends playable, verified in the browser, with the old art as a fallback for anything not yet replaced.

---

## 1. What changes, and what doesn't

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Characters, creatures | 16px string sprites, 2–3 frames, one body for every human | PixelLab characters: real walk, idle, and action cycles in 3 directions (west is east mirrored) |
| Terrain | Procedural fills per tile (flat colors, a few speckles) | PixelLab Wang tilesets per region, drawn on a **dual grid** so walls, floors, and water blend at the corners, plus generated decals |
| Props and stations | `Painter` shapes | Generated sprites for every node, station state, landmark, and decoration |
| Icons | 10×10 strings | 16×16 generated icons |
| Portraits | `Painter` busts | Generated 40×40 busts, with mouth and blink frames edited in by hand |
| Title and intro | Procedural skylines and panels | Generated paintings, with the same animated overlays on top |
| Effects, lighting, HUD text, telegraphs | Code | **Stay code.** They're crisp, readable, tuned, and tested. They get recolored to the new palette, not replaced. |

**Unchanged on purpose:** the 16px tile grid, the 480×270 base view, collision, every map's layout, every tuning number, and the two-layer drawing (pixels on the low-res canvas, text on the UI canvas). Readability rules stay law: telegraphs are code-drawn blinks, cones, and circles over any sprite; awareness icons keep shape plus color.

**The design decision this reverses.** DESIGN §1 chose "art drawn in code" for consistency, no pipeline, tiny builds, and testable sprites. The overhaul keeps those properties a different way: one style guide and shared references keep it consistent; a small TypeScript pipeline (no runtime dependencies) packs it; the PNGs add roughly 1–2 MB; and `tests/art.test.ts` checks every sheet the way `validateSprite` checked strings.

## 2. Style guide

Everything below is the brief for every prompt and the checklist for every review.

- **Scale:** authored at 1:1 game pixels and never scaled in the engine. No mixels.
- **View:** low top-down 3/4 (the classic RPG angle). Characters face the camera for "south". Walls show a south-facing front face.
- **Outline:** a single dark outline (near `#0e0f0d`) on characters, creatures, props, and icons. Terrain is lineless.
- **Light:** from the top left. Three or four tones per material, cool shadows, warm highlights.
- **Palette:** the existing `PAL` is the backbone: damp stone greys and greens, rust, timber, and sludge. Accents are reserved and mean something:
  - goblin green `#7fbf5a` (the player and goblins)
  - glowcap lime `#a6e05a` (food, the Cistern's glow)
  - slime green `#58c078` (gel, slimes, the generator)
  - copper `#c87533` (machines, the Pumpworks, quest parts)
  - brass `#f0d070` (buttons, plaques, the Charter)
  - home orange `#f0a040` (the Burrow and the Cooperative as they warm up)
  - danger red `#e05040` (only hostile things: the Matron's markings, the `!` icon)
- **Mood:** the world is serious, damp, and old. The goblin and the Burrow are the warmest, most saturated things on screen.
- **Text:** never generated. Signs, plaques, the statue, and notices are blank boards with text drawn by code, so it stays legible and editable.

## 3. Technical design

### Runtime
- **Files:** packed PNGs live in `src/assets/art/` and are imported through `import.meta.glob`, so Vite hashes them and the relative `base: './'` keeps working on GitHub Pages.
- **Loading:** `loadArt()` decodes every image at boot, next to `loadFonts()`. Nothing draws until it resolves. Tests never call it.
- **Metadata:** `src/render/art/sheets.gen.ts` is generated by the pack script: per sheet, the file, cell size, anchor (the pixel between the feet), and named clips (`walk_s`, `walk_n`, `walk_e`, `idle_s`, …) with frame counts. Hand-tuned values (fps, offsets) live next to it in `src/render/art/sheets.ts`.
- **`SheetSprite`** (`src/render/sheet.ts`) has the same contract as today's `Sprite`: anchor-based `draw(ctx, clip, frame, x, y, { flip, silhouette, alpha })`. Silhouettes (hit flash, telegraph blink, focus outline) are cached per frame with `source-in`. Entity code changes one call at a time.
- **Fallbacks:** if a sheet isn't loaded, the renderer uses the old string sprite or `Painter` prop. Every phase ships with the rest of the game still drawn the old way.
- **Terrain:** `src/render/terrain.ts` draws a **dual grid**: display tiles sit half a tile off the map grid, and each one picks its Wang tile from the four map cells at its corners (wall, floor, or water). Thin walls, corners, and pools all fall out of the corner rules. Crawl pipes, gates, holes, exits, and landmarks draw as overlays on top. Terrain is still baked once per region load, so frame cost doesn't change.

### Pipeline (TypeScript, no dependencies, Node 22 runs it directly)
- `art/sources.json` lists every asset: its PixelLab id, tool, prompt, and the frame URLs to pull. It's the record of what was generated and how to regenerate or extend it.
- `npm run art:fetch` downloads new frames into `art/raw/` (committed, so packing never depends on PixelLab URLs staying up).
- `npm run art:pack` crops each character's frames to one shared box, finds the anchor, packs clips into one sheet per asset, slices tilesets with their corner metadata, and writes `sheets.gen.ts`.
- `scripts/art/png.ts` is a small PNG reader and writer on `node:zlib`.

### Tests (`tests/art.test.ts`)
- Every creature behavior, human id, item, node, station state, and landmark has a sheet entry or a declared fallback.
- Every sheet file exists, and its PNG size (read from the header) matches its cells and frames.
- Anchors sit inside their cells. Every clip the renderer asks for exists.
- Each tileset has a tile for all 16 corner combinations.

## 5. Map by map

Each map gets its own tileset, decals, and landmark art. Lighting darkness values stay as they are and get re-checked against the new tiles.

### The Burrow — *NOT A LAIR*
- **Floor:** packed brown earth, worn smooth in a path from the passage to the rug. **Walls:** rough earth and fieldstone with roots coming through.
- **Story it has to tell:** forty years of someone living here. The chalk tallies, the house drawing, the rug, cobwebs, and small rubbish stay procedural decals, recolored.
- **Upgrade states, all visible:** boarded holes leaking green light → brick patches with a brass plaque; sack bed → Fewer Springs mattress; candle stub → scrap lantern; broken workbench → repaired; empty spots → mushroom beds (empty, sprouts, growing, ready), drying rack (empty, loaded), grinder, generator (off, on) with copper pipes; sign → DEFINITELY NOT A LAIR; the decorations.
- **Mood:** cold green at the start, warming to orange. The tiles must look good under both.

### Refuse Galleries — *where the city puts things it has finished with*
- **Floor:** grimy grey flagstones with rubbish in the cracks. **Walls:** soot-dark brick with chute mouths.
- **Landmarks:** the fallen chandelier in the rat nest; the city access grate with its ladder and daylight; junk piles, crates, and hiding heaps.
- **Decals:** paper, broken glass, bones, rags, puddles.

### Fungus Cistern — *damp, green, and growing*
- **Floor:** wet green-grey flagstones. **Walls:** big mossy blocks with water stains. **Water:** dark sludge pools with a wet lip (its own tileset, chained to the floor so they match).
- **Landmarks:** the Founder's statue standing in the east pool, crowned with glowcaps; the sluice gate and Hobb's post; glowcap patches, truffle roots, slime pools.
- **Decals:** moss tufts, small mushrooms, drips, algae.

### Old Pumpworks — *closed indefinitely, unidentified clanking*
- **Floor:** riveted iron plates, rust streaks, drain grates. **Walls:** iron panels with copper and steel pipes.
- **Landmarks:** the seized pistons; copper machinery; the reinforced scrap pile; the drain gate.
- **The Pump Room:** the same set, darker, with the two steam valves and the vents. The Matron is the brightest, reddest thing in it.

### Goblin Cooperative — *Office of Burrow Charters*
- **Floor:** warm wooden planks. **Walls:** plaster over a wood skirting. The sock bunting stays procedural.
- **Furniture:** Grubb's desk, the Hatch window, the Activity Board, the queue rope with no queue, sconces.
- **Mood:** lamplit and warm, the most "civilized" room in the game.

### Title, intro, and map screen
- The title and the six intro panels get paintings (A8). The map screen (M) stays a drawn node graph; it gets the new palette.

## 6. Process

1. **Anchor first.** Nothing else is generated until the goblin and one tileset pass review in the engine.
2. **Same words every time.** Every prompt carries the style line: *"dark damp dungeon, muted earthy palette, single color dark outline, low top-down 3/4 view"*. Props and creatures pass the goblin as a style reference where the tool takes one.
3. **Review in the game, not in a gallery.** Each asset is checked at 1× under the region's lighting, next to the goblin, moving.
4. **Reject when:** it doesn't read at 1×; the silhouette is mushy; the outline or light direction is off; it's the wrong size; its colors fight the palette; or an animation pops between frames. Rejected assets get one retry from their phase's line, then the reserve.
5. **Commit per phase,** with the ledger updated.

### Lessons so far
- A v3 character's figure fills its canvas: ask for the figure height you want, not a bigger canvas.
- Raised (cliff) tilesets don't reliably paint their low ground. Chain them to the region's floor tile (`lower_base_tile_id`) so it matches, and draw floors as their own layer underneath.
- Standard raised sets have 25 tiles and miss the diagonal steps the maps use; the pack script builds those from half tiles, and `tests/art.test.ts` proves every map resolves.
- Template animations sit the character about 3px higher on the canvas than its rotation images: anchor from an animation frame.
- An object's ZIP names its animation folders after the description, not `display_name`: rename them to `walk_<dir>` after pulling.
- For props, a 1-direction review pack is the bargain: 64 candidates at 42px or less (16 up to 85px) for 10 generations, returned in `item_descriptions` order, so four tries of 16 props come back as 16 rows of four. The largest style image sets the canvas, so pad references to the size you want.
- Generated objects fill their canvas. Pick the canvas from the prop's size (16 for a letter, 24 for a crate, 32 for a bed, 48 for a landmark), because pixel art can't be scaled afterwards.
- `edit_image_pixen` makes a prop's other states (searched, harvested, repaired) at 1 generation and keeps the composition, but it won't change proportions. Widen by hand instead.
- Lettering needs about 4px a letter in the 3×5 font. Check that a sign's board fits its words before picking it; desks, hatches, and the statue went without.
- Review packs price by canvas tier, not a flat 10: 24, 32, and 48px cost 10, but 40px cost 15.
- Review packs make good portraits too: describe a "portrait bust" per slot and the eight tries of each come back on-model and in one style. Pick busts with the mouth shut and the eyes visible, so code can open the one and close the other. Find the eyes from their whites, and take the lid color from the cheek, not from above the eye (that's brow, hair, or a spectacle frame).
- Belly-up and other big pose changes need blunt wording ("only its pale underbelly, the shell hidden"); a gentle prompt keeps the original pose.
- Pixen paints whole scenes well, up to 640×380 for one generation, but it picks the composition. Generate the parts that need placing separately (a sky, an underground), say where the focal point goes ("just right of the middle"), reroll any that put it at an edge, and measure the marks on the packed image afterwards.
- Paintings can't zoom: the intro's push-in would smear them, so painted panels hold still and the overlays carry the motion. Keep anything animated above the letterbox (y 224).
